# Part I — Engineering Design Methodology

Every system in this book, from a sensor node to a microgrid, is built by one chain of work: problem to requirements, requirements to architecture, architecture to models and prototypes, prototypes to a verified, documented product that someone else can build again. Part I teaches that chain as a method, before the later parts apply it to instrumentation, embedded devices, robots, factories, energy systems, communications and software. Its rule runs through the whole book: every decision is traced to a number written down before the build, and every number is closed by a test with a pass/fail limit. Two small running systems carry the method. Chapters 1–3 follow **CCM-1**, a battery-powered remote temperature monitor for vaccine refrigerators, from a one-sentence brief to a validated architecture. Chapters 4–5 follow **PM-4**, a four-channel 12 V DC power monitor for server racks, through prototypes, tests, calibration, release and a field return.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How to state a problem with evidence and a value, find its stakeholders, and write functional and nonfunctional requirements that are measurable and verifiable.
- Why boundaries and constraints (regulations, networks, shipping rules, budgets) must be listed on day one.
- How requirements are allocated into design specifications and budgets for error, latency, energy and cost, with margins, and traced through design to acceptance tests.
- How a system is decomposed into functions and blocks, partitioned between hardware and software, and described by interfaces and by its flows of data, energy and control.
- How trade studies are run honestly (constraints first, scores from evidence, weights tested for rank reversal) and recorded as decisions.
- How models are built to answer a stated question, simulated with solvers that cannot step over the physics, fitted to data, tested for sensitivity, validated against independent data and made reproducible.
- How each prototype build retires named risks, and how instruments, test plans, calibration, diagnosis and regression tests turn it into evidence.
- How schematics, BOMs, design calculations, test reports and revisions are kept consistent under version control, so that a shipped unit can be traced and rebuilt.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

Part I owns the method; later chapters use it without repeating it. Requirements tables, budgets with margins, component-selection tables, verification tables and FMEAs appear in every chapter of Parts II–VIII, and the Part IX projects follow the full chain end to end, with Chapter 40 turning it into a capstone report template. Chapter 3's modeling workflow is developed further in Chapters 30 and 33 (scientific computing, digital twins) and applied in Chapter 39, and Chapter 5's documentation practice in Chapter 29.

The method rests on theory owned by the sibling books, cited in one line and used. The mathematics of least squares, sensitivity, uncertainty propagation, optimisation and numerical solvers is in *The Mathematics Universe*, [Ch. 22–25](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/engineering-mathematics/blob/main/part-7-numerical-computational/25-mathematical-modeling.md), and the statistics behind acceptance tests in its [Ch. 20](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/engineering-mathematics/blob/main/part-6-probability-statistics/20-statistical-analysis.md). Lumped thermal and electrical networks come from *Engineering Physics*, [Ch. 17](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Engineering-Physics/blob/main/CHAPTERS/CHAPTER%2017.md), and dynamic-system modeling from *Circuits, Signals and Communications*, [Ch. 33](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Circuits-Signals-and-Communications/blob/main/part-7-control-system-fundamentals/33-dynamic-system-modeling.md). Embedded architecture, partitioning, energy budgets and firmware validation come from *Electronics and Computing*, [Ch. 25](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electronics-and-Computing/blob/main/part-6-embedded-and-real-time-systems/25-embedded-system-architecture.md) and [Ch. 28](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electronics-and-Computing/blob/main/part-6-embedded-and-real-time-systems/28-embedded-system-design.md). Battery equivalent circuits, installation design and standards compliance come from *Electrical Energy and Machines*, [Ch. 16](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-4-power-generation/16-energy-storage.md) and [Ch. 30](https://github.com/The-Studio-of-Sadiq/Electrical-Energy-and-Machines/blob/main/part-8-electrical-safety-and-engineering-practice/30-electrical-system-design.md).
