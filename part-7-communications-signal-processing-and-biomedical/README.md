# Part VII — Communications, Signal Processing, and Biomedical Applications

Parts II–VI built systems that measure, compute, move and convert energy. This part follows the *information* they produce: across the air from a node to a gateway, through filters and detectors on an embedded processor, and from the most demanding measurement chain of all, one connected to a human body. The three chapters share one idea. A signal arrives with a budget against it: path loss and fading on a radio link, noise and interference in a sensor channel, electrode offset, mains pickup and leakage current on a patient. Each chapter writes that budget down per stage, closes it with a stated margin, and tests it in the configuration the system will really be used in.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How an RF front end is assembled around a transceiver IC — PA, filters, switch, LNA, reference oscillator, antenna — and how each block's loss or noise appears in the link budget.
- Why antennas are chosen for the installation rather than the data sheet.
- How a link budget is built from a measured path-loss model, how a fade margin follows from the shadowing statistics, and how spreading factor, airtime, regulatory duty cycle and battery life trade against each other.
- How a wireless sensor network is chosen, how collisions and retries set its reliability, and how co-sited transmitters are kept from blocking a receiver.
- How a sensor's noise is reduced before and after the converter, how filters, spectra and features are chosen for a specific physical mechanism, and how a detector is specified by its detection probability and false-alarm rate.
- How a DSP algorithm, including audio and image processing, is moved to an embedded processor with a latency budget and a bit-exact reference model.
- How biopotentials such as ECG and EMG are acquired through electrodes, instrumentation amplifiers and driven-right-leg circuits, how mains interference and motion artefact are rejected, and how basic features (heart rate, QRS detection, muscle activation) are extracted.
- How patient isolation, leakage-current limits and the IEC 60601 family shape a biomedical front end, and what regulatory and ethical obligations come with data taken from people.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

Chapter 26 sits under the IoT systems of Chapter 11, which own the layers above the radio (MQTT, provisioning, security), and it feeds the complete wireless sensor network project of Chapter 38. Chapter 27 takes the measurement chains of Chapters 7 and 8 (signal conditioning, ADC selection, data acquisition) and adds the processing; its features and detectors are the inputs to the machine-learning methods of Chapter 32 and the edge inference of Chapter 12, and its embedded DSP runs inside the energy monitor of Chapter 35. Chapter 28 applies the sensor and conditioning methods of Chapters 6 and 7 to the human body, uses Chapter 27's filtering and detection on biopotentials, and adds the safety discipline that no other application in the book demands to the same degree.
