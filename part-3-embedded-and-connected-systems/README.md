# Part III — Embedded and Connected Systems

Part II built measurement chains that end in a number. This part puts a processor at the end of the chain and makes it act: drive a motor, join a network, and finally decide on its own what its sensors are telling it. Its three systems are a battery-powered radiator valve, a cold-chain monitoring fleet for 120 pharmacies, and a vibration node that classifies a water pump's condition. None of their field failures came from a broken part. A valve that emptied its batteries overnight, an alarm that never reached anyone and a classifier that called healthy pumps unbalanced all failed in the joins, at an assumption about a battery, a connection or a scale factor that was never written down and tested. Each chapter carries its system from requirements through budgets, component choice and implementation to verification, failure analysis and the revision the failures forced.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How a microcontroller is chosen by eliminating candidates on hard constraints before comparing the rest, and how flash and RAM are budgeted for updates and growth.
- How sensors and an actuator are interfaced so that the faults that matter are detected, with safety-relevant reactions in hardware.
- How firmware is partitioned between hardware, interrupts and threads, and how an end-to-end latency budget is written across all of them.
- Why battery life is a charge budget but brownout is a source-resistance problem, and how both are tested at the corners where they meet.
- How a fleet is partitioned between device, gateway and cloud, and how connectivity and payloads are chosen against a data budget.
- What MQTT and HTTP each guarantee, and how an alarm path is made to survive silent network failures.
- How devices are provisioned, authenticated and authorised, and kept from collapsing a broker when they all reconnect at once.
- When computation belongs at the edge, and how a learned model is deployed, quantised, validated on held-out machines and guarded against inputs it has never seen.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book
