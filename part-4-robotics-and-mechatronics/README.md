# Part IV — Robotics and Mechatronics

A robot is where the rest of this book meets in one machine: sensors and acquisition from Part II, a microcontroller and firmware from Part III, motors and power electronics from the sibling books, and a mechanism that turns it all into force and motion. It is also where integration mistakes become physical: a shoulder sized at the wrong pose drops a tube, a wheel radius 1.5 % off sends a robot into a wall 60 m later, a bus with nowhere to put regenerated energy lets a robot roll down a ramp. Part IV calculates such failures before the build. Its systems are a six-axis laboratory arm, a hospital delivery robot and its motor-control board, a bridge-inspection quadcopter, and a collaborative assembly arm taken through integration and field test, each carried from requirements through budgets, part selection and implementation to verification, failure analysis and the second version the failures forced.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How a task sets a robot's architecture and joint count, and how frames, kinematics, the Jacobian and singularities serve as integration tools.
- How joint actuators are sized at the worst pose with acceleration and duty, and how error budgets decide gears, encoders and compensation.
- How a wheeled robot estimates its pose, how odometry error grows and is calibrated, and how planning, localization, stopping fields and navigation are sized.
- How drivers, encoder interfaces, sensor fusion and cascaded loops are implemented on an embedded controller, and why a loop belongs where its worst-case delay fits its phase margin.
- How power distribution handles regeneration and brown-out, and which faults may retry and which must latch.
- How a multirotor is sized and tested, and how a robot is integrated, calibrated, identified and tested in the loop and in the field.

## How this part connects to the rest of the book

Part IV applies the method of Part I (requirements, architecture, modelling, test plans) to the sensors of Part II and the embedded practice of Part III; Chapter 15 reuses Chapter 10's MCU selection, timing and fault handling. Inside the part, Chapter 13 owns kinematics and actuator selection, Chapter 14 differential drive, odometry and line following, and Chapter 15 motor drivers, encoders, PID implementation and robot buses; Chapter 17 integrates them. Forward, the line-following robot of Chapter 34 and the motor drive of Chapter 37 apply them as complete projects; safety functions belong to Chapter 21, battery packs to Chapter 23 and model calibration to Chapter 33.
